## The short answer, and the honest one

Is it legal to download Facebook videos? The honest answer is that it depends entirely on what you download and what you do with it. Saving a video is not automatically illegal, but copyright law and Facebook's own terms set clear boundaries. Downloading your own content or content you have permission to use is generally fine. Downloading someone else's video and republishing it as your own is not. This guide walks through where the lines sit so you can save clips with confidence and avoid trouble.

This is a plain-language explanation, not formal legal advice. Rules vary by country, and if you are doing anything commercial with downloaded video, consult a professional. But for everyday personal use, the principles below will keep you safe.

## What you can generally download safely

There are several situations where downloading a Facebook video is clearly reasonable and low risk. In each case, either you hold the rights or you have permission.

- **Your own videos.** Anything you created and posted is yours. Downloading your own uploads to back them up or repurpose is completely fine.
- **Content you have permission to use.** If a creator explicitly allows you to save and reuse their video, or grants you a license, you are in the clear. Keep a record of that permission.
- **Public domain or freely licensed content.** Some videos are shared under licenses that permit reuse, sometimes with conditions like attribution. Check the terms and follow them.
- **Personal, private viewing.** Saving a public video to watch later on your own device, without redistributing it, is a low-risk personal use in most contexts.

The common thread is respect for ownership. When you either own the content or have clear permission, downloading is straightforward.

## Where the risk begins

The trouble starts when you take someone else's work and treat it as your own. Copyright protects the creator of a video by default, even when it is posted publicly. Public visibility is not the same as free-to-use. Reuploading another person's video, editing it into your own content without permission, or using it commercially can all constitute infringement.

There are also platform rules to consider. Facebook's terms of service govern how content on the platform may be accessed and used, and mass or automated scraping of content is generally prohibited. Downloading a clip you have rights to for personal use is a very different thing from harvesting large volumes of other people's videos.

The safest mental test is simple. Ask yourself two questions: do I own this, or do I have permission, and am I about to republish it as if it were mine? If you own it or have permission, and you are not passing off someone else's work as your own, you are almost always fine. If not, stop and get permission first.

## Practical rules for staying safe

You do not need to be a lawyer to stay on the right side of this. A few habits cover most situations.

**Get permission before reusing.** If you want to repost or build on someone else's video, ask. Many creators are happy to say yes, and a short message protects you.

**Always credit the original creator.** Even when reuse is permitted, crediting the creator is both courteous and often required by the license. It also protects your own reputation.

**Do not claim others' work as your own.** This is the clearest line. Passing off someone else's video as yours is both an ethical and a legal problem.

**Respect private and restricted content.** If a video is not public, it is not meant to be saved. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is it illegal to download Facebook videos?

Downloading is not automatically illegal, but it depends on the content and your use. Saving your own videos or content you have permission to use is fine, while downloading someone else's video to republish as your own can infringe copyright.

### Can I repost a Facebook video I downloaded?

Only with the creator's permission. Public visibility does not mean a video is free to reuse, so ask the original creator, follow any license terms, and always give credit before reposting.

### Is it okay to download a public Facebook video for personal use?

Saving a public video to watch later on your own device, without redistributing it or claiming it as your own, is generally a low-risk personal use. Avoid downloading private or restricted content, which is not meant to be saved.
