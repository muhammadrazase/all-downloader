Twitch clips are the internet's highlight reel. A perfect flick, a clutch round, a streamer losing their mind at a jump scare, they all live inside those 60-second bites. The problem is that Twitch keeps clips locked to its own player, and there is no obvious "save" button. If you want a clip on your camera roll or your desktop, you need a workaround.

Here is how to **download Twitch clips** on both mobile and PC, plus a few things worth knowing before you hit save.

## Why there's no built-in download button

Twitch treats clips as sharable links, not files. You can copy a URL, embed it, or drop it in a Discord chat, but the platform never hands you an actual MP4. That design keeps everything inside Twitch's ecosystem, where it can serve ads and track views.

For creators and viewers that is frustrating. If you want to edit a clip into a montage, post it to TikTok, or just keep it before a streamer deletes their VODs, you need the raw file. That is where a downloader comes in.

## The fastest way: use a clip downloader

The simplest method works the same on any device. Grab the clip link, paste it into a tool, and download the file.

1. Open the clip on Twitch and click the **Share** icon.
2. Choose **Copy Link**. The URL usually looks like `clips.twitch.tv/...` or `twitch.tv/<channel>/clip/...`.
3. Paste it into our [Twitch clip downloader](/twitch-clip-downloader).
4. Pick your quality and tap download.

The file saves as a standard MP4 with no watermark, so you can edit or repost it freely. It works in any browser, which means the exact same steps apply whether you are on a laptop or a phone.

## Downloading on mobile (iPhone & Android)

Phones make this slightly trickier because Twitch's app hides the direct link behind a share sheet, but it still works.

**On iPhone:**

- In the Twitch app, tap the clip, then the share arrow.
- Tap **Copy Link** instead of choosing an app.
- Open Safari, go to the downloader, and paste the link.
- After the file generates, press and hold the video, then choose **Download** or **Save to Files**. From there you can move it to Photos.

**On Android:**

- Tap the share icon on the clip and select **Copy Link**.
- Open Chrome and paste the link into the downloader.
- Tap download. Most Android browsers drop the MP4 straight into your Downloads folder.

If the clip opens inside the Twitch app instead of copying, look for the three-dot menu, that almost always has a plain **Copy Link** option.

## Downloading on PC (Windows & Mac)

Desktop is the easiest experience because you have a real browser and a file system.

- Open the clip in your browser and copy the URL from the address bar, or use the Share button.
- Paste it into the downloader and choose a resolution. Higher resolutions are better if you plan to edit.
- The MP4 lands in your Downloads folder, ready for a video editor.

Desktop is also where you should download anything you plan to use in a project. Editing from a full-quality source keeps your final export sharp instead of soft and blocky.

## A note on quality

Clips are captured at whatever resolution the streamer broadcast in, so a 1080p60 stream gives you a crisp clip while a lower-bitrate stream will look softer no matter what tool you use. Always pick the highest resolution offered, then downscale later if you need a smaller file. You can shrink a video without losing much, but you can never add detail that was not there to begin with.

## Save clips before they disappear

This is the part people learn the hard way. Twitch clips can vanish. If a channel gets banned, a VOD expires, or a streamer clears old content, the clips tied to that footage can go with it. If a moment matters to you, download it now rather than assuming the link will work next month.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it legal to download Twitch clips?

Downloading for personal use, like keeping a highlight or clipping your own gameplay, is generally fine. If you plan to repost someone else's clip publicly, credit the original creator and check whether they allow reuse. Twitch content is still owned by the streamer.

### Why won't my clip link work in the downloader?

Usually it is the wrong link. Make sure you copied the clip URL (containing `/clip/` or `clips.twitch.tv`) and not the channel or VOD link. If the clip was deleted or the channel was removed, the source no longer exists and cannot be downloaded.

### Can I download in 1080p?

Yes, as long as the streamer broadcast in 1080p. The download quality can never exceed the original stream quality, so a clip from a 720p stream tops out at 720p.
