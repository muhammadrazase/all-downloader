## Content ideas that consistently perform

Some formats reliably earn attention because they match how people scroll. Use these as templates and adapt them to your niche.

- **The quick tutorial.** Teach one specific thing in under a minute. "How to fix a squeaky door" beats "home maintenance tips." Specific and useful travels far.
- **Before and after.** Transformations hook instantly because the payoff is visual and immediate. Renovations, cleaning, cooking, fitness, and design all thrive here.
- **The myth or mistake.** Open with a common belief and correct it. "You are storing your knives wrong" earns a stop because it challenges the viewer directly.
- **Behind the scenes.** Show the process, not just the polished result. People trust and follow creators who let them see how the work happens.
- **Listicle in motion.** "Three tools every beginner needs" works as a fast, punchy sequence with each item as its own beat.
- **React and explain.** Take a trending clip or question in your field and add genuine expertise. Value plus timeliness is a strong combination.

The thread connecting all of these is a promise made in the first two seconds and delivered before the viewer scrolls away.

## The trends shaping distribution

Beyond individual ideas, a few broad trends define what works in 2026. Ignore them and even good content underperforms.

**Short vertical is the default.** Reels-style vertical clips get the widest distribution. Even longer content benefits from a vertical, mobile-first cut. Design for a thumb scrolling a phone, not a viewer sitting at a desk.

**The hook is everything.** The first two to three seconds decide whether a clip lives or dies. Lead with the payoff, a bold claim, or a striking visual. Do not warm up, and never open with a long logo animation.

**AI-assisted production is normal.** Automatic captions, translation, and quick editing tools have lowered the barrier to publishing polished video. Captions in particular are no longer optional, since most people watch on mute.

**Series beat one-offs.** Repeatable formats build recognition and train the algorithm to distribute your content. A named recurring series gives viewers a reason to follow rather than just watch once.

**Authenticity over gloss.** Overproduced ads underperform against honest, human clips. A phone-shot video with a real person and a clear point often beats an expensive production with no soul.

## Frequently asked questions

### What type of Facebook video gets the most reach in 2026?

Short vertical clips with a strong hook in the first few seconds get the widest distribution because the recommendation system favors them. Specific, useful formats like quick tutorials, transformations, and myth-busting clips consistently perform best.

### How long should Facebook videos be?

For maximum reach, keep most clips under a minute and lead with the payoff immediately. Longer videos can work when the content justifies it, but a mobile-first vertical cut with captions almost always distributes better.

### Do I need expensive equipment to make good Facebook videos?

No. Authentic, phone-shot video with a clear idea and a strong hook often outperforms polished, overproduced content. Captions, consistency, and a repeatable format matter far more than expensive gear.
