## What actually happened on Facebook in 2025

If 2025 had one theme, it was video pulling the whole platform toward it. Reels stopped being a bolt-on feature and became a default surface. The feed increasingly opened with short vertical clips, autoplay was smoother, and the line between Reels, Stories, and regular video posts blurred. For everyday users this meant more time inside the app watching, and for creators it meant a genuine chance to reach people beyond their follower list.

The second big story was artificial intelligence moving from the background to the foreground. Recommendation quality improved noticeably, so feeds felt more relevant even when they showed content from accounts you had never interacted with. Creation tools also matured: automatic captions, suggested cuts, background cleanup, and text-to-video experiments lowered the barrier to publishing polished clips.

A quieter but important shift was the continued balance between friends content and recommended content. Facebook spent 2025 tuning how much of your feed came from people you know versus creators the system thought you would enjoy. The result was a feed that felt more like a personalized media channel than a friend timeline, which pleased some users and frustrated others.

## The numbers behind the trend

Rather than chase specific figures, it is more useful to note the direction. Watch time on short video kept climbing, creator publishing volume grew, and the share of feed impressions coming from recommendations rose over the year. These trends are consistent with what the platform has publicly emphasized and with how the product visibly changed.

## Predictions for 2026

Looking ahead, several developments seem likely based on the clear trajectory of the platform. None of these are guarantees, but each follows logically from where things already stand.

- **AI creation becomes standard, not novelty.** Expect built-in tools that generate captions, translate audio, restyle footage, and suggest edits to become a normal part of posting rather than an experiment.
- **Reels and video posts converge further.** The distinction between a Reel and a regular video will keep fading, with a unified vertical video experience that surfaces the best clip regardless of how it was originally posted.
- **Smarter, more personal recommendations.** The feed will lean harder on understanding individual taste, which raises the ceiling for new creators but also makes early engagement signals more important than ever.
- **Deeper assistant integration.** Conversational AI inside the app will help with search, summaries, and content discovery, changing how people find video and pages.
- **Better creator monetization paths.** As video volume grows, expect clearer ways for creators to earn, tied to watch time and genuine engagement rather than raw follower counts.

## Frequently asked questions

### What was the biggest Facebook change in 2025?

The biggest shift was the platform becoming truly video-first, with Reels moving to the center of the feed and AI powering both recommendations and creation tools. Feeds increasingly showed recommended video from creators rather than only updates from friends.

### Will Facebook rely more on AI in 2026?

Yes, the clear direction points toward deeper AI use in 2026, from more personalized recommendations to built-in creation features like automatic captions, translation, and editing. Conversational assistants inside the app are also likely to expand.

### Is short video still the best format for reach on Facebook?

Short vertical video remains the format with the widest distribution because it fits how the recommendation system and feed now work. Consistency and a strong opening hook matter more than expensive production for reaching new audiences.
