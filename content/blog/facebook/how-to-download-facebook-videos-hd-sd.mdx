## Why you might want to save a Facebook video

Facebook is now full of video worth keeping. A tutorial you want to follow later, a recipe from a page you love, a highlight from your own upload, or a clip you have permission to reuse. The problem is that Facebook does not offer a universal save-to-device button for every video, and clips scroll out of reach quickly. Learning how to download Facebook videos in HD and SD solves that, and it works the same way whether you are on a phone or a computer.

Before we start, one ground rule. Only download videos you own, videos that are clearly free to reuse, or content you have explicit permission to save. We cover the details in our guide on whether it is [legal to download Facebook videos](/blog/facebook/is-it-legal-to-download-facebook-videos), and it is worth reading before you save anything that is not yours.

## The fastest method: an online downloader

The simplest approach that works everywhere is a web-based tool. You copy the video link, paste it into the tool, and pick your quality. There is nothing to install, and it behaves identically on Android, iPhone, Windows, and Mac.

Here is the full flow using a [Facebook video downloader](/facebook-video-downloader):

- **Step 1 — Copy the link.** On the video, tap or click the three-dot menu, or the share icon, and choose "Copy link." On desktop you can also copy the URL from the address bar when viewing the video directly.
- **Step 2 — Paste it into the tool.** Open the downloader and paste the link into the input box.
- **Step 3 — Choose HD or SD.** The tool detects available formats. Pick HD for the sharpest copy or SD to save space and data.
- **Step 4 — Download.** Tap the download button and the file saves to your device.

This method is best for most people because it requires no app, respects your storage, and gives you a clear choice between HD and SD every time.

## Downloading on mobile (Android and iPhone)

On mobile the online method above is usually the smoothest path, but the save behavior differs slightly by platform.

On **Android**, after you tap download, the file typically lands in your Downloads folder or directly in Gallery, depending on your browser. If your browser asks where to save, choose a folder you will remember. You can then move the clip into any app or gallery album.

On **iPhone**, Safari handles downloads a little differently. When you tap download, the file may go to the Files app first. From there, open Files, find the video in Downloads, and use the share sheet to "Save Video" into your Photos library. This extra step is normal on iOS and takes only a few seconds.

For both platforms, choose HD when the video quality matters, such as a tutorial where small text needs to stay readable, and SD when you are short on space or on a slow connection.

## Downloading on PC (Windows and Mac)

On a computer the process is even easier because file management is straightforward. Open the video on Facebook, copy its link, paste it into the downloader, select HD or SD, and click download. The file saves to your Downloads folder by default, and you can drag it anywhere from there.

Desktop is the better choice when you plan to edit a clip, archive several videos, or need the highest quality copy for a project. Larger screens also make it easier to confirm you grabbed the right video and the right resolution before saving.

## HD or SD: which should you pick?

The choice comes down to purpose. Pick **HD** when you want the clearest image, plan to watch on a big screen, or intend to edit or repost with permission. HD files are larger but preserve detail and readability.

Pick **SD** when you want a smaller file, are saving many videos, or are on limited data or storage. SD looks perfectly fine on a phone screen and downloads faster. A good rule of thumb: HD for keeping and editing, SD for quick reference and casual viewing.

## Troubleshooting common issues

If a download fails, the usual culprit is the link. Make sure you copied the direct video link and not a link to a page, group, or profile. Reopen the video, use the share menu, and select "Copy link" again.

Private videos are another common blocker. If a video is set to private or restricted, a downloader cannot access it, and that limit exists for good reason. Only public videos, your own uploads, or content you have rights to will be available.

Finally, if the video plays but will not save on iPhone, remember the extra step: it often downloads to Files first, and you then save it into Photos manually. This is an iOS behavior, not a fault with the tool.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I download a Facebook video in HD?

Copy the video's link using the share or three-dot menu, paste it into an online Facebook video downloader, and select the HD option before tapping download. HD gives you the sharpest copy, which is ideal for tutorials or editing.

### Can I download Facebook videos on my iPhone?

Yes. Copy the video link, paste it into a web-based downloader in Safari, and download it. On iPhone the file often saves to the Files app first, so open Files and use "Save Video" to move it into your Photos library.

### Why won't a Facebook video download?

The most common reasons are copying the wrong link or trying to save a private video. Make sure you copied the direct video link, and remember that only public videos, your own uploads, or content you have permission to use can be downloaded.
