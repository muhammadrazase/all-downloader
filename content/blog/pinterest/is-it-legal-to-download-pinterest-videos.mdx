It's one of the most common questions people ask before saving a clip: **is it legal to download Pinterest videos?** The honest answer is that it depends on what you download and, more importantly, what you do with it afterward.

This guide breaks it down in plain language. It's not legal advice, but it will help you understand the difference between a harmless personal save and something that could get you in trouble.

## Two separate rules are in play

When you download a Pinterest video, two different things apply at once:

1. **Copyright law**, which protects the person who created the video.
2. **Pinterest's Terms of Service**, which is the agreement you accept by using the platform.

Something can technically break Pinterest's terms without being a copyright crime, and vice versa. Understanding both keeps you clear on where the real risks are.

## Personal use is the safe zone

In most places, saving a video to watch offline for yourself sits comfortably in the low-risk category. If you download a recipe to follow in the kitchen, a workout to do at the gym, or a tutorial to reference later, you aren't distributing anything or profiting from someone's work.

Copyright issues almost always arise from **what happens next**, not from the act of saving. A file sitting on your phone for personal reference is very different from that same file uploaded to another platform.

Where personal use stays safe:

- Watching a video offline yourself.
- Keeping a reference clip for a project you're working on.
- Saving a tutorial so you don't lose it if the creator deletes it.

## Where it crosses the line

The risk climbs sharply once other people or money enter the picture. These uses can infringe copyright regardless of how you obtained the file:

- **Re-uploading** the video to Pinterest, TikTok, Instagram, or YouTube as if it were yours.
- **Commercial use**, like putting the clip in an ad, a paid course, or a product.
- **Removing credit** or presenting someone else's work under your own name.
- **Mass redistribution**, such as sharing downloaded videos in a public group or channel.

None of these become acceptable just because the video was freely viewable. Public visibility is not the same as permission to reuse.

## What Pinterest's terms say

Pinterest's Terms of Service ask users not to collect or scrape content in ways that go around the platform's normal features. Downloading an occasional video for yourself is a mild, personal action; running automated tools to harvest thousands of videos is the kind of behavior those terms target.

Breaking the terms isn't a criminal matter, but it can cost you access to your account. Pinterest can suspend or remove accounts that violate its rules, so it's worth keeping your usage reasonable and personal.

## Fair use, briefly

In some countries, limited copying is allowed under doctrines like **fair use** (United States) or **fair dealing** (UK, Canada, Australia). These can cover things like commentary, criticism, news, research, or education.

Fair use is not a free pass, though. It's judged case by case on factors like how much you use, whether your use is transformative, and whether it harms the original creator's market. If you're relying on it for anything public, it's worth getting proper advice rather than assuming you're covered.

## How to download responsibly

You can stay well within safe territory by following a few habits:

- **Keep downloads personal.** If only you will ever see the file, you're almost always fine.
- **Ask before you reshare.** Many creators happily grant permission when asked directly.
- **Always credit** the original creator if you're allowed to reshare.
- **Never remove watermarks** or claim someone's work as your own.
- **Use a clean tool** that doesn't add its own watermark or bundle unwanted software.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it illegal to download a Pinterest video for myself?**
In most cases, no. Saving a publicly visible video to watch offline for personal use is low-risk. The legal problems usually start when you republish or commercialize someone else's work.

**Can I get in trouble for reposting a downloaded Pinterest video?**
You can. Re-uploading a creator's video without permission may infringe their copyright, and it can also violate the terms of whatever platform you post it to. Ask the creator first, and credit them.

**Does downloading break Pinterest's rules?**
Occasional personal downloads are minor, but Pinterest's terms discourage large-scale scraping and collecting content outside its normal features. Keep your usage personal and reasonable to avoid account issues.
