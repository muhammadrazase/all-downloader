LinkedIn doesn't give you a save button on videos. If you've found a native LinkedIn video worth keeping — a conference talk, a product demo, or your own post — you can still get it onto your iPhone using Safari and the built-in Files app. No third-party app to install, and nothing to sign up for.

This guide covers native LinkedIn videos only (clips uploaded directly to LinkedIn). Reshared YouTube or Vimeo links aren't LinkedIn-hosted, so they won't work the same way.

## Before you start

A few things worth knowing up front:

- **iOS won't let you long-press a LinkedIn video to save it.** The feed player blocks the save gesture, which is why you need a downloader.
- **Downloads land in the Files app**, not your Photos library, until you move them.
- **Respect the creator.** Downloading for offline viewing or personal reference is fine; reposting someone's work as your own is not.

## Step 1: Copy the video's post link

Open the **LinkedIn app** on your iPhone and find the post with the video.

1. Tap the **three dots (•••)** in the top-right corner of the post.
2. Tap **Copy link to post**.

That copies a clean URL to your clipboard. If you're browsing LinkedIn in Safari instead of the app, tap the post's timestamp to open it on its own page, then copy the address from the URL bar.

## Step 2: Paste it into the downloader

Open **Safari** and go to our [LinkedIn video downloader](/linkedin-video-downloader).

1. Tap the input box and **paste** the link (touch and hold, then choose Paste).
2. Tap the **Download** button.
3. Wait a second or two while the video is fetched, then pick your quality — **HD (1080p)** if it's offered.

The tool pulls the direct video file so you get the actual MP4, not a screen recording.

## Step 3: Save the file to your iPhone

When the download link appears, tap it. Safari on iOS 15 and later handles this cleanly:

1. Safari shows a **Download** prompt — tap **Download** to confirm.
2. A small **downloads icon** appears next to the address bar. Tap it to watch progress.
3. Once it finishes, tap the file in that list, then tap the **share icon** and choose **Save to Files**.
4. Pick a location — **On My iPhone > Downloads** is a safe default — and tap **Save**.

That's it. The MP4 is now stored locally and plays offline.

## Step 4 (optional): Move it into Photos

If you'd rather have the video in your camera roll:

1. Open the **Files** app and find the MP4.
2. **Touch and hold** the file, then tap **Share**.
3. Choose **Save Video**.

It now shows up in the Photos app, ready to trim, share, or send in Messages.

## If the download doesn't start

A blocked download is almost always one of these:

- **You copied the wrong link.** Make sure you used *Copy link to post*, not *Copy profile URL* or a comment link.
- **It's not a native video.** If the clip is an embedded YouTube or Vimeo player, LinkedIn is only linking to it. Download it from the original platform instead.
- **Safari blocked the pop-up.** Go to **Settings > Safari** and turn off **Block Pop-ups**, then try again.
- **Private or members-only post.** Videos behind a login wall or in a private group can't be fetched.

## A note on quality

Native LinkedIn videos are usually uploaded at 720p or 1080p. You can't get a higher resolution than the creator originally posted — no tool can add detail that isn't there. If HD is available, take it; otherwise the standard version is your best copy.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I download a LinkedIn video directly in the LinkedIn iPhone app?

No. LinkedIn's app has no save-video option. You need to copy the post link and use a browser-based downloader like the one above, then save the file through Safari and the Files app.

### Where do downloaded LinkedIn videos go on iPhone?

By default they save to the Files app — usually under **On My iPhone > Downloads**. From there you can move the video into your Photos library with the Share > Save Video option if you want it in your camera roll.

### Is it legal to download LinkedIn videos on my iPhone?

Downloading a public video for personal, offline use is generally fine. Re-uploading someone else's video, claiming it as your own, or using it commercially without permission is not. When in doubt, ask the creator.
