## The year video took the front seat

If you want honest LinkedIn 2026 predictions, start with what actually happened in 2025. That was the year native video stopped being a side experiment and became a core part of how the feed works. Short vertical clips earned their own swipeable experience, and professionals who had never touched a camera started posting to camera weekly.

The change was not only cosmetic. The distribution logic shifted too. Well-made video began surfacing to viewers well beyond a creator's immediate network, which meant a single strong clip could introduce an expert to thousands of new professionals in a way that text posts rarely did.

## What defined LinkedIn in 2025

Several themes stood out across the year, and each one sets up what comes next.

- **Native video everywhere.** Uploads, a dedicated video feed, and better in-app editing pushed motion to the center of the experience.
- **The creator economy matured.** More professionals treated LinkedIn as a primary channel, not a backup to other platforms, and consistent posting became a genuine career asset.
- **AI writing assistance spread.** Draft suggestions and rewriting tools became common, which raised the volume of content and, in turn, raised the value of authentic human voices.
- **Newsletters and long-form held steady.** Even as video surged, subscription-based newsletters kept giving experts a durable audience they owned.

The combined effect was a feed that rewarded people who showed up consistently with a real point of view, in whatever format suited the message.

## Predictions for 2026

No one can predict a platform perfectly, but the direction of travel is clear enough to plan around.

### Video becomes the default expectation

Expect video to move from "nice to have" to "assumed." Professionals building authority will be expected to appear on camera at least occasionally, much as having a written profile became non-negotiable a decade ago. The bar for production will stay refreshingly low, since audiences value clarity and credibility over polish.

### Discovery keeps widening

The feed will likely keep pushing strong content beyond immediate connections. This favors creators who make genuinely useful clips, because reach will depend more on quality signals and watch time than on who already follows you. It also means a single good video can still change a career in an afternoon.

### AI raises the floor and the value of authenticity

As AI-assisted content grows, generic posts will blend together. The counterintuitive result is that unmistakably human material, specific stories, real opinions, and on-camera presence, will stand out more, not less. Personal credibility becomes the moat.

### Creators lean on repurposing

Smart creators will treat one recording as raw material for many posts. A single talk becomes clips, quotes, and a written breakdown. Saving and studying strong videos from others will be part of that workflow, which is why a dependable [LinkedIn video downloader](/linkedin-video-downloader) has become a common tool in a creator's kit.

## Frequently asked questions

### What was the biggest LinkedIn change in 2025?

The biggest change was the rise of native short-form video and a dedicated video feed. This shifted distribution so strong clips could reach far beyond a creator's existing network, turning video into a primary way for professionals to build authority.

### Will text posts still work on LinkedIn in 2026?

Yes. Text and long-form newsletters remain effective, especially for depth and owned audiences. The realistic expectation is a mix, where video handles reach and first impressions while written content builds durable relationships with the people who already follow you.

### How should a beginner start with LinkedIn video in 2026?

Begin with one short, unpolished clip per week that explains something you know well. Keep it under two minutes, speak plainly to the camera, and add a written hook. Consistency and clarity matter far more than production quality when you are starting out.
