Twitter, now X, is full of clips worth keeping: news moments, sports highlights, tutorials, and reaction GIFs you want to reuse later. The problem is that X gives you no built-in save button. Tap and hold a video and you get playback options, not a file.

This guide shows you exactly how to **download Twitter videos** and GIFs on mobile and PC. The method is the same everywhere: copy the post link, paste it into a downloader, pick a quality, and save. No account, no software, no fuss.

## First: copy the tweet link

Every method below starts with the URL of the post that contains the video. Here is how to grab it.

- **On mobile:** Open the tweet, tap the **share icon** (the small arrow), then tap **Copy link**.
- **On desktop:** Click the **share icon** under the post and choose **Copy link to post**. You can also copy the URL straight from your browser's address bar.

A valid link looks like `https://x.com/username/status/1234567890`. That long number at the end is the tweet ID, and it is all the downloader needs.

## Download Twitter videos on a phone (iPhone & Android)

Phones don't allow direct video saves from the X app, so a browser-based tool does the work.

1. Copy the tweet link using the steps above.
2. Open your browser and go to a [Twitter video downloader](/twitter-video-downloader).
3. Paste the link into the box and tap **Download**.
4. Choose your quality. Higher resolution means a larger file.
5. Tap the download button to save the MP4.

**On iPhone**, the file lands in your **Files** app by default. Open Files, find the video, then tap the share icon and choose **Save Video** to move it into Photos.

**On Android**, the MP4 usually saves straight to your **Downloads** folder and appears in your Gallery within a minute. If it doesn't show, open your file manager and check the Download directory.

## Download Twitter videos on PC or Mac

The desktop flow is even quicker because you have a full keyboard and a real Downloads folder.

1. Copy the post link from your browser.
2. Open the downloader in a new tab.
3. Paste the link and press Enter.
4. Select the resolution you want, up to the highest the original upload supports.
5. Click download. The MP4 saves to your Downloads folder.

From there you can drag it into a video editor, a presentation, or a Google Drive folder. Desktop is also the best place to grab longer clips, since large files download faster on a stable connection.

## How to save a Twitter GIF

Here is the twist most people miss: **Twitter GIFs are not actually GIFs.** When you upload a GIF, X converts it to a silent looping MP4 to save bandwidth. So when you "download a GIF," you're really downloading a short video file.

The same downloader handles this automatically. Paste the tweet link, and the tool detects the animated clip and offers it as an MP4. If you specifically need a true `.gif` file for a chat app or a document, save the MP4 first, then run it through any free MP4-to-GIF converter.

## Quick tips for cleaner downloads

- **Pick the right quality.** For sharing on other social apps, 720p is plenty and keeps files small. For editing or archiving, grab the highest resolution.
- **Private accounts won't work.** Downloaders can only reach public posts. If the account is protected, there's no public link to pull from.
- **No watermarks.** A good tool returns the original file exactly as uploaded, with nothing stamped on top.
- **Check the source.** Only download from clips you have a right to use. Reposting someone else's video without credit can break platform rules and copyright law.

## Why not just screen-record?

Screen recording works in a pinch, but it's a poor substitute. You lose resolution, you capture your own interface bars, and the audio can drift out of sync. A direct download pulls the original MP4 at full quality, which matters if you plan to edit or repost the clip. Recording should be your last resort, not your first move.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need an app to download Twitter videos?

No. A browser-based downloader works on any device with an internet connection. Installing a separate app usually just adds ads and permissions you don't need, so a web tool is the cleaner choice for a one-off save.

### Where do downloaded Twitter videos go on my phone?

On iPhone, files typically save to the **Files** app, and you can move them into Photos with **Save Video**. On Android, they go to your **Downloads** folder and usually appear in your Gallery automatically.

### Can I download videos from private or protected accounts?

No. Downloaders only work with public posts that have a shareable link. If an account is set to protected, its videos are not publicly accessible and cannot be pulled by any tool.
