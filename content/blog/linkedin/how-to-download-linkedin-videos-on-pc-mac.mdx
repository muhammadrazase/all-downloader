Downloading a LinkedIn video on a computer is the easiest version of this task. You've got a full browser, a real Downloads folder, and no mobile quirks to work around. LinkedIn still hides any save button, but copying the post link and pasting it into a downloader takes about twenty seconds on Windows or macOS.

This applies to native LinkedIn videos — clips uploaded directly to LinkedIn. Reshared YouTube or Vimeo videos are hosted on those platforms and should be downloaded from the original source.

## What you need

- Any browser: **Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari**.
- The URL of the LinkedIn post with the video.
- Nothing else — no software, no extension, no account.

## Step 1: Copy the video's post link

On **LinkedIn.com**, find the post that contains the video.

1. Click the **three dots (•••)** in the top-right corner of the post.
2. Click **Copy link to post**.

That copies a clean URL to your clipboard. Alternatively, click the post's timestamp to open it on its own page, then copy the full address from the browser's URL bar — both work.

## Step 2: Paste the link into the downloader

Open a new tab and go to our [LinkedIn video downloader](/linkedin-video-downloader).

1. Click the input box and **paste** the link (**Ctrl + V** on Windows, **Cmd + V** on Mac).
2. Click **Download**.
3. Wait a moment while the video is fetched, then choose your quality. Pick **HD (1080p)** if it's listed.

The tool retrieves the direct MP4 file, so you get the original video rather than a re-encoded screen recording.

## Step 3: Save the MP4 to your computer

When the download link appears, click it.

**On Windows:**
- The file saves to your **Downloads** folder by default.
- Watch progress in the download bar (Chrome/Edge) or the downloads panel (Firefox).
- If your browser is set to ask, choose a folder and click **Save**.

**On Mac:**
- Safari and Chrome save to **Downloads** automatically.
- Click the **downloads icon** near the toolbar to see the file.
- If nothing downloads in Safari, right-click the download link and choose **Download Linked File**.

Open the folder, double-click the MP4, and it plays in your default media player.

## Step 4 (optional): Organize or convert

Once the file is on disk you have full control:

- **Rename it** something meaningful so you can find it later.
- **Move it** into a project folder or cloud drive.
- **Trim or convert** it with any desktop editor if you only need a clip or a different format.

Having the raw MP4 locally is exactly why the desktop route is the most flexible.

## If the download won't start

Most failures trace back to one of these:

- **Wrong link.** Use *Copy link to post*, not a link to the author's profile or an individual comment.
- **Not a native video.** An embedded YouTube or Vimeo player isn't LinkedIn-hosted. Download from that platform instead.
- **Pop-up blocker.** If your browser blocked the download, allow pop-ups for the downloader site and retry.
- **Login-gated post.** Videos in private groups or behind a sign-in wall can't be fetched.

## A word on quality

Native LinkedIn video is typically uploaded at 720p or 1080p. You can only download the resolution the creator originally posted — no tool can add detail beyond the source. Choose HD when it's offered; otherwise the standard version is the best available copy.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I download a LinkedIn video without any software on my PC or Mac?

Yes. You copy the post link, paste it into a browser-based downloader, and save the MP4 — all in your normal browser. There's nothing to install and no account to create.

### Where does the LinkedIn video save on my computer?

By default it goes to your **Downloads** folder on both Windows and Mac. If your browser is set to prompt for a location, you'll choose the folder yourself before the file saves.

### Can I download my own LinkedIn videos to reuse them?

Yes. If you posted the video, downloading your own MP4 to repurpose or archive is completely fine. For other people's videos, keep it to personal or offline use and get permission before republishing.
