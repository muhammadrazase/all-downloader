## From a dorm room to the world's front page

The history of Facebook begins in February 2004, when a Harvard student project called "TheFacebook" opened to a single campus. It was a directory with photos and status lines, nothing more. Yet within a year it spread to other universities, then to high schools, and by 2006 to anyone over thirteen with an email address. That decision to open the doors turned a campus tool into a global habit.

What made the early product stick was not technology but psychology. Facebook mapped real identities onto real relationships, so the network felt honest in a way anonymous forums did not. People used their true names, tagged real friends, and shared real moments. That trust became the foundation for everything the platform built later.

## The years that shaped the platform

Several turning points defined the first decade. Each one added a new layer that we still recognize in the product today.

- **2006 — News Feed.** Instead of visiting each profile, users saw a single stream of updates. It was controversial at launch but became the template for every social feed since.
- **2007 — Platform and apps.** Third-party developers could build on top of Facebook, creating games and tools that pulled in millions of new users.
- **2009 — The Like button.** One click captured intent, emotion, and data all at once, and it reshaped how the web measured engagement.
- **2012 — Mobile and the IPO.** Facebook went public and, more importantly, bet its future on smartphones. It also acquired Instagram that year.
- **2014 — WhatsApp and Oculus.** Two purchases signaled ambitions far beyond a single website: messaging at global scale and a stake in immersive computing.

By the mid-2010s Facebook was no longer one app. It was a family of products touching billions of people across messaging, photos, and eventually payments.

## The pivot to video and creators

Around 2015 the company recognized that attention was moving to moving pictures. It pushed native video hard, launched Facebook Live, and later introduced Watch as a home for longer content. The rise of short vertical video, driven by competition, forced another reinvention. Reels arrived as a direct answer to changing habits, and the feed slowly rebalanced around discovery rather than just friends. The algorithm learned to recommend content from strangers, which opened the door for new creators to reach large audiences without buying ads.

## Rebrand, AI, and the road to 2026

In 2021 the parent company became Meta, signaling that Facebook the app was now one part of a larger vision spanning social, hardware, and immersive experiences. The name change drew skepticism, but it clarified strategy: the blue app would keep serving billions while newer bets developed alongside it.

The most consequential recent shift has been artificial intelligence woven directly into the experience. Recommendation systems grew far more precise, creation tools began generating captions and edits automatically, and assistants started answering questions inside the apps. By 2026 Facebook feels less like a static feed and more like a personalized media service that also happens to connect you with people you know.

Through all of this, the core remains recognizable. A profile, a feed, a network of relationships, and a growing library of video. ## Why the history still matters

Understanding this timeline helps you use the platform better today. Every feature you rely on, from the feed to Reels to save tools, exists because of a specific decision in this story. Knowing why News Feed replaced profile visits, or why video became central, explains the incentives behind what the algorithm shows you now.

It also explains the ongoing tension between friends and creators. The platform constantly balances content from people you chose to follow against content it thinks you will enjoy. That balance is the living result of twenty years of experiments, and it will keep shifting as habits change.

## Frequently asked questions

### When was Facebook founded?

Facebook was founded in February 2004 as "TheFacebook," originally limited to Harvard University. It expanded to other colleges within months and opened to the general public in September 2006, which began its rapid growth into a global network.

### Why did Facebook change its name to Meta?

The parent company rebranded to Meta in 2021 to reflect a broader focus beyond the single Facebook app, including messaging, immersive experiences, and hardware. The Facebook app itself kept its name and continues to serve billions of users.

### How did Facebook shift toward video?

Facebook began prioritizing video around 2015 with native uploads, Facebook Live, and the Watch tab. It later added Reels to compete in short vertical video, and by 2026 video and AI-driven recommendations sit at the center of the experience.
