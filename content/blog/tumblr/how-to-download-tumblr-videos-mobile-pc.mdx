Tumblr is still one of the best corners of the internet for niche art, animation loops, fandom edits, and video posts you won't find anywhere else. The problem is that Tumblr doesn't give you a save button. When you find a clip worth keeping, there's no obvious way to pull it down to your device.

This guide shows you exactly how to **download Tumblr videos** on both mobile and desktop, using the fastest method that actually works in 2026.

## Why Tumblr makes downloading tricky

Tumblr videos are embedded in a custom player, and the file itself is hosted on Tumblr's own media servers. When you tap and hold on the video, you usually get playback controls or nothing at all, not a download option. That's by design. The platform wants views to happen inside the app or site, not on your camera roll.

The workaround is simple: you copy the link to the post, and a downloader tool extracts the actual video file for you. No screen recording, no quality loss, no watermark stamped across the frame.

## The fastest way: use a Tumblr video downloader

The most reliable method on any device is a browser-based tool. Nothing to install, and it works the same whether you're on an iPhone, an Android phone, or a laptop.

Here's the full process with our [Tumblr video downloader](/tumblr-video-downloader):

1. **Open the Tumblr post** with the video you want, either in the app or in your browser.
2. **Copy the post link.** In the app, tap the share icon (the paper airplane or arrow) and choose **Copy link**. On the web, copy the URL from your address bar.
3. **Paste the link** into the downloader's input box.
4. **Press Download.** The tool reads the post and pulls out the video file.
5. **Save the video** to your device. On desktop it lands in your Downloads folder; on mobile it saves to your camera roll or Files app.

The whole thing takes about ten seconds once you have the link. Because the tool grabs the original file, you keep the full resolution the uploader posted.

## Downloading on a PC or Mac

Desktop is the smoothest experience because you have a real browser and a proper file system.

- Find the video post on **tumblr.com**.
- Click the three-dot menu on the post, or copy the URL straight from the address bar.
- Paste it into the downloader and hit Download.
- Choose your quality if offered, then save.

One tip: if a post has multiple videos or a photoset mixed with a clip, make sure you're copying the permalink to the specific post. Click the post's timestamp to open it on its own page, then copy that URL. This avoids grabbing the wrong media.

## Downloading on Android and iPhone

Mobile works just as well, it's just a couple of extra taps to save the file.

**On Android**, after the download finishes, the file usually drops straight into your Gallery or Downloads folder. If your browser asks where to save, pick Downloads and you'll find it in your Files app afterward.

**On iPhone**, tap the download link, then use **Save to Files** or long-press the finished video and choose **Save to Photos**. iOS is a little stricter about where downloads go, so the Files app is your friend here.

## Keep the quality high

A few habits make a real difference:

- **Always start from the original post,** not a reblog screenshot or a cross-posted version. The closer you are to the source, the better the file.
- **Skip screen recording.** It captures your interface, drops frames, and produces a much larger file for worse quality.
- **Download over Wi-Fi** for larger clips so you're not waiting on a slow connection or burning mobile data.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a Tumblr account to download videos?**

No. As long as the post is public, you can copy its link and run it through a downloader without logging in. Private or password-protected blogs are a different story, since the content isn't publicly accessible.

**Will the video have a watermark?**

No. A good downloader pulls the original file exactly as it was uploaded, so there's no added watermark or branding. What the creator posted is what you get.

**Why won't my link work?**

The most common cause is copying a link to the blog's home page instead of the specific post. Open the individual post first by tapping its timestamp, then copy that permalink and paste it in again.
