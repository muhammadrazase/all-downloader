## How the Facebook algorithm actually works in 2026

The biggest change for creators is simple to state and important to internalize: reach now comes from recommendations, not just your follower count. In 2026 the Facebook algorithm decides how far a piece of content travels based on how people respond to it, especially in the first hours after posting. A page with a few thousand followers can reach far more people if a clip earns strong early signals, and a large page can flop if a post lands flat.

The system watches a handful of behaviors closely. It looks at whether people watch a video to the end, whether they rewatch it, whether they react, comment, share, and save, and how quickly those signals arrive. Strong early engagement tells the algorithm the content is worth showing to a wider, non-follower audience. Weak signals cap distribution to your existing followers or less.

This is why chasing followers is the wrong first goal. The right goal is making content that earns engagement, because engagement is the fuel that unlocks reach. ## Reels are your distribution engine

If you want reach in 2026, Reels are where you get it. Short vertical video receives the widest recommendation distribution on the platform, which means Reels are the surface most likely to put you in front of new people. Treat them as your primary format, not an afterthought.

A few practical rules make Reels work harder for you:

- **Hook in the first two seconds.** Lead with the payoff, a bold claim, or a striking visual. If the opening is slow, the clip dies before the algorithm ever considers it.
- **Design for sound-off viewing.** Most people watch on mute, so burn in captions and make the visual tell the story on its own.
- **Keep it tight.** Cut every second that does not earn its place. Pacing and completion rate matter more than length.
- **End with a reason to engage.** A question, a surprising turn, or a clear takeaway prompts comments and shares, which feed distribution.

## Consistency beats intensity

One great video will not build a page. A steady rhythm of good videos will. The algorithm rewards creators who publish reliably because consistent posting gives the system more chances to find your audience and more data to understand who enjoys your content.

Pick a cadence you can actually sustain, whether that is three times a week or once a day, and hold it. It is far better to post four solid clips a week for months than to burn out publishing daily for two weeks and then vanish. Consistency also compounds: each post teaches the algorithm more about who to show your work to, so distribution tends to improve over time for creators who stick with it.

Build repeatable series rather than one-off posts. A named, recurring format gives viewers a reason to follow and trains the system to distribute your content to people who engaged with earlier episodes.

## Read your data and adapt

The creators who grow fastest treat their analytics as a feedback loop. After each post, look at what the numbers tell you. Which clips held attention to the end? Which earned shares and saves? Which lost people in the first few seconds? Those answers point directly to what your specific audience wants.

Studying your own top performers, and strong videos from others in your niche, sharpens your instincts. When you find a clip worth breaking down, save it and analyze the structure of its hook and pacing. The key is to let data, not ego, drive decisions. If a format you love keeps underperforming, respect the signal and adjust. If a format you were unsure about takes off, lean into it.

## Engagement is a two-way street

Finally, remember that engagement is something you participate in, not just something you receive. Reply to comments quickly, especially in the first hour when early activity carries the most weight. Ask genuine questions, respond to your community, and give people a reason to return. Pages that feel like a conversation earn loyalty, and loyal audiences produce the fast, strong signals the 2026 algorithm rewards.

Put simply: make Reels with strong hooks, post consistently, read your data, and engage with your community. Do those four things well and reach follows. The platform is more open to new creators than it has ever been, because distribution is earned by content, not bought by follower count.

## Frequently asked questions

### How does the Facebook algorithm decide reach in 2026?

The algorithm distributes content based on early engagement signals like watch time, rewatches, reactions, comments, shares, and saves rather than follower count alone. Strong early signals unlock wider recommendation distribution to non-followers.

### Are Reels better than regular videos for growth?

Yes, Reels and short vertical video receive the widest recommendation distribution, making them the best format for reaching new audiences. They should be your primary format, with a strong hook in the first two seconds and captions for sound-off viewing.

### How often should creators post on Facebook?

Post at a consistent cadence you can sustain, such as three to five times a week, rather than posting intensely and then disappearing. Consistency gives the algorithm more opportunities to find your audience and improves distribution over time.
