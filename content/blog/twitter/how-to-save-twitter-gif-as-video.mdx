You found the perfect reaction GIF on Twitter, now X, and you want to keep it. But there's no save button, and even when you grab it, the file comes out as a video instead of a GIF. That's not a bug. It's how the platform works.

This guide explains why that happens and shows you exactly how to **download a Twitter GIF** as a clean MP4, then turn it back into a real GIF if you actually need one.

## Why Twitter GIFs are really videos

Here's the part that trips everyone up. When you post a GIF on X, the platform doesn't store it as a `.gif` file. It converts it into a short, silent, looping **MP4 video**.

Why? GIFs are heavy and inefficient. A few seconds of animation as a true GIF can be several megabytes, while the same clip as MP4 is a fraction of the size and looks sharper. X made the swap years ago to save bandwidth and speed up feeds.

So when you set out to "download a Twitter GIF," what you're really pulling is that underlying MP4. For most uses, that's exactly what you want.

## How to download a Twitter GIF on mobile

The process is the same on iPhone and Android, because it all happens in the browser.

1. Open the tweet that has the GIF.
2. Tap the **share icon** and choose **Copy link**.
3. Open a [Twitter video downloader](/twitter-video-downloader) in your browser.
4. Paste the link and tap **Download**.
5. The tool detects the animated clip and offers it as an MP4. Save it.

**On iPhone**, the MP4 goes to your **Files** app. To move it into Photos, open Files, tap the share icon, and choose **Save Video**.

**On Android**, the file usually saves to your **Downloads** folder and shows up in your Gallery shortly after.

## How to download a Twitter GIF on PC

Desktop is the fastest route, especially if you plan to convert the file afterward.

1. Copy the tweet link from your browser's address bar or the share menu.
2. Open the downloader in a new tab.
3. Paste the link and press Enter.
4. Choose the quality and click download.
5. The MP4 saves to your Downloads folder, ready to use or convert.

## How to turn the MP4 back into a real GIF

Sometimes you genuinely need a `.gif` file, for example to drop into a Slack message, a wiki, or an email signature that doesn't accept video. Here's how to convert it.

- **Online converters:** Upload your MP4 to any free MP4-to-GIF site, then download the result. Quick and no install required.
- **Desktop tools:** Free apps like ffmpeg or a video editor can export a GIF with more control over frame rate and size.
- **Watch the file size.** A true GIF is much larger than the MP4. Keep the clip short and the dimensions modest, or the file can balloon fast.

For everyday sharing on X, Discord, or WhatsApp, skip the conversion entirely. The MP4 loops and plays just fine, and it's smaller and cleaner than a GIF would be.

## When to keep the MP4 and when to convert

Use this quick rule of thumb:

- **Keep the MP4** if you're reposting on social media, editing it into another video, or sending it through any modern chat app. They all handle MP4 natively.
- **Convert to GIF** only if your destination refuses video, like some forums, older wikis, or certain email clients.

Most of the time, the MP4 is the better file. It's smaller, sharper, and universally supported.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my Twitter GIF download as an MP4?

Because X stores GIFs as MP4 video files, not as true GIFs. When you download one, you get that underlying MP4. It loops silently just like the GIF did, and it's smaller and sharper. Convert it to `.gif` only if your destination doesn't accept video.

### Can I get a true .gif file directly from Twitter?

Not directly. The platform doesn't host `.gif` files, so any tool has to hand you the MP4 first. To end up with a real GIF, save the MP4 and run it through a free MP4-to-GIF converter.

### Does downloading a Twitter GIF add a watermark?

No. A good downloader returns the original file exactly as it was uploaded, with no watermark or branding added. The clip keeps its original quality.
