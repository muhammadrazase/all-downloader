## Why saving LinkedIn videos is genuinely useful

Learning how to download LinkedIn videos is more practical than it sounds. A conference talk, a product walkthrough, or a sharp two-minute explainer can be exactly the reference you want to revisit offline, share with a teammate, or study frame by frame while preparing your own content.

The catch is that LinkedIn does not offer a built-in save-to-device button for most native video. The play button lets you watch, but keeping a local copy takes a couple of extra steps. Below is the clean way to do it on both mobile and desktop, along with the etiquette that keeps you on the right side of the rules.

## Before you start: get the video URL

Almost every download method needs the direct link to the post that contains the video. On desktop, click the timestamp or the three-dot menu on the post and choose "Copy link to post." On mobile, tap the same three-dot menu and select "Copy link."

That URL is the key ingredient. It points to the specific post, and a downloader tool reads it to locate the underlying video file. Keep it on your clipboard for the steps that follow.

## Downloading on desktop or PC

The most reliable path on a computer is a web-based tool that accepts the post link.

- **Step 1.** Open the LinkedIn post in your browser and copy the post link using the three-dot menu.
- **Step 2.** Open a trustworthy [LinkedIn video downloader](/linkedin-video-downloader) in a new tab.
- **Step 3.** Paste the link into the input field and start the process.
- **Step 4.** Choose your preferred quality when offered, then save the MP4 to your computer.

This browser method needs no software installation, works across Windows, macOS, and Linux, and keeps the original resolution when the source allows it. Because nothing is installed, it is also the safest option for a work laptop where you cannot add new programs.

## Downloading on mobile

Phones add one wrinkle. Mobile browsers handle downloaded files differently, and the LinkedIn app itself does not expose a save button. The approach is nearly identical to desktop, with a small adjustment.

- **Step 1.** In the LinkedIn app, tap the three-dot menu on the post and choose "Copy link."
- **Step 2.** Open your mobile browser, such as Chrome or Safari, and go to the downloader tool.
- **Step 3.** Paste the link, start the process, and pick your quality.
- **Step 4.** Tap and hold the resulting video, or use the browser's download prompt, to save it to your camera roll or Files app.

On iPhone, saved videos usually land in the Files app first, and you can then move them to Photos. On Android, they typically appear in your Downloads folder or gallery straight away.

## Keeping quality high

Two habits protect video quality. First, always choose the highest resolution offered, since a downloader can only save what the source provides. Second, avoid re-compressing the file by running it through multiple converters. One clean save beats three lossy ones.

If a video looks soft, it is usually because the original upload was low resolution, not because of the download. There is no way to add detail that was never there, so start from the best available source clip.

## Staying within the rules

Downloading for personal reference, offline viewing, or private study is generally reasonable. Redistributing someone else's video, stripping their name, or reuploading it as your own is not, and it can breach both copyright and LinkedIn's terms.

The simple rule of thumb is credit and permission. If you plan to do anything public with a saved clip, ask the creator first and attribute them clearly. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I download any LinkedIn video?

You can generally save native videos that play directly in the feed by copying the post link and using a downloader tool. Private videos, videos behind restricted access, or externally embedded clips may not be accessible, and you should always respect the creator's rights before reusing anything.

### Does downloading reduce the video quality?

A downloader saves the quality of the original upload, so choose the highest resolution offered and avoid running the file through extra converters. If a clip looks soft, that usually reflects a low-resolution source rather than the download process itself.

### Where do downloaded videos go on my phone?

On iPhone, saved files often appear in the Files app first and can be moved to Photos. On Android, they usually land in your Downloads folder or gallery. The exact location depends on your browser's download settings.
